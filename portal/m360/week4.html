Week Brief

A resume is a targeted evidence document.

Your goal is to make fit easy to see for one role family, posting, or opportunity type. Relevant evidence moves forward; noise moves out.

TargetChoose a specific role, posting, or opportunity type.
Read signalsIdentify repeated skills, tools, responsibilities, outcomes, and behaviors.
Show evidenceReplace duty-only language with statements you can support.
Stay accurateAlign honestly. Never invent credentials, tools, titles, or proficiency.

Learn It

Make the strongest relevant proof visible first.

Targeting begins with the opportunity and ends with evidence a reader can understand and you can defend.

01
Evidence document

Your resume is not your full life story.

Ask what opportunity you are aiming at, what evidence proves relevant capability, and what the reader should remember first.

02
Opportunity signals

Repeated language tells you what to prioritize.

Look for recurring skills, tools, responsibilities, outcomes, and behaviors. Use those signals to decide what belongs and what can be reduced.

03
Evidence beats duties

Show contribution and capability.

Duty-only language explains what you were assigned. Evidence-based language explains what you did, how you worked, and what capability the statement demonstrates.

04
Evidence integrity

Targeting never requires exaggeration.

Coursework, labs, projects, and prior experience can support emerging skills when labeled honestly. If you cannot defend a claim in an interview, do not put it on the resume.

Evidence can come from:technical labs and projects · troubleshooting and support scenarios · investigation or analysis work · documentation · customer or stakeholder work · operations · coordination · process improvement · leadership · project delivery
Live Instruction

Use class and coaching to move from target language to proof.

Session 1 teaches the targeting model. Session 2 is hands-on coaching in the Practice It workspace.

Session 1 · Instruction · 60 min

Read the target and identify the evidence that belongs.

  • Identify 5–8 target signals.
  • Compare target language with your existing evidence.
  • Recognize duty-only versus evidence-based statements.
  • Check every claim for accuracy.
Session 2 · Coaching · 60 min

Revise the resume and prepare the Week 4 proof.

  • Refine the targeted resume draft.
  • Revise at least three evidence bullets.
  • Make keyword use natural and supportable.
  • Finish the targeting note and gap reflection.
